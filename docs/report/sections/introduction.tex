\section{What? Why? How?}
\label{section:what}

\textbf{What.} We take a policy, such as the EU AI Act, GDPR or a company's own AI policy, and the code of an AI
application. For every obligation in the policy, we decide whether the application meets it. Each answer must point to
its proof: a file and the lines in it. This is \emph{compliance-as-code}: compliance is checked automatically, like unit
tests or Open Policy Agent rules~\cite{opa}.

\textbf{Why.} The AI Act gives different duties to systems with different risk levels, and its text is often vague: it
says what to achieve, not how~\cite{veale2021demystifying}. The proof is spread over code, configuration and documents.
A manual audit is out of date after the next commit. Simple text search misses things, and LLMs sometimes invent
facts~\cite{ji2023survey}.

\textbf{How.} One pipeline prepares the inputs: the policy becomes requirements, the repository becomes evidence, and
the risk tier decides which requirements apply. Then three methods decide each requirement: fixed rules, an LLM judge
that reads retrieved evidence~\cite{lewis2020rag}, and a hybrid of the two.

\textbf{First results.} The hybrid is the most accurate (86.1\%, against 79.4\% for the rules and 71.5\% for the LLM)
at 14\% of the cost of the LLM alone. But it keeps the rules' 12.3\% false-compliance rate. The LLM alone finds every
gap, but it is too strict.

\section{Paper structure and original contribution(s)}
\label{section:structure}

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item a simple, precise definition of the task: give each requirement a status, a confidence and the evidence behind
        it (Chapter~\ref{chapter:problem});
  \item a pipeline with three interchangeable decision methods and a citation check (Chapter~\ref{chapter:approach});
  \item a benchmark of five AI applications with 225 labels, including traps for rules and LLMs, and a comparison of
        the three methods with significance tests (Section~\ref{section:numericalValidation}).
\end{itemize}
Chapter~\ref{chapter:related} reviews related work, Chapter~\ref{chapter:approach} gives the approach, results and
discussion, and Chapter~\ref{chapter:concl} concludes and lists the planned work.
