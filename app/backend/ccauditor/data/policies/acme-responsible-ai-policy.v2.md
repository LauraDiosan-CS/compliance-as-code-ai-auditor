# ACME Responsible AI Engineering Policy (ACME-POL-AI-001), revision 2

Scope: every software product built or operated by ACME Corp (and its subsidiaries) that embeds a
machine-learning model or calls a third-party AI model ("AI system"). The policy is binding for
product engineering, data science and platform teams. Exceptions require a written waiver from the
AI Governance Board, recorded in the product repository under `docs/waivers/`.

Normative keywords: "MUST" = mandatory control, audited; "SHOULD" = expected unless a documented
reason exists.

Changes in revision 2: POL-3 log retention raised from 180 to 365 days; POL-7 extended from adverse
decisions to all automated decisions with significant effect and requires four-eyes approval for
rejections; new POL-12 on watermarking of generative AI output; POL-2(2) upgraded from SHOULD to MUST.

## POL-1 | Disclosure of AI use in user interfaces

### 1

Any user interface (web, mobile, e-mail, chat, API documentation for external integrators) in which
a person interacts with an AI system, or is shown a score, ranking, classification or text produced
by an AI system, MUST clearly disclose that AI is involved. The disclosure MUST be visible at or
before the first interaction and MUST NOT be hidden behind a tooltip or the terms of service only.

### 2

Where an AI output is shown to staff as decision support (for example a ranking or a risk score),
the interface MUST state that the output is an AI-generated recommendation and who takes the final
decision.

## POL-2 | Labelling of AI-generated content

### 1

Text, images, audio or video generated by an AI system and delivered to customers or the public MUST
be labelled as AI-generated in a way that is visible to the recipient (for example an "AI-generated"
badge next to a chatbot reply or a footer line in an e-mail).

### 2

Generated content MUST additionally carry a machine-readable marker (for example a response header,
a metadata field or an embedded content credential) so downstream systems can detect it. See POL-12
for media content.

## POL-3 | Prediction logging and log retention

### 1

Every prediction, score, classification or generated response produced in production MUST be logged
with at least: timestamp (UTC), request or correlation id, model name and version, a reference to the
input (an input hash or record id, not raw personal data where avoidable), the output, and, where a
human acted on it, the reviewer's identity and action.

### 2

Prediction logs MUST be retained for at least 365 days and MUST be protected against modification
(append-only storage or equivalent). Shorter retention requires a waiver.

## POL-4 | Retention of personal data in raw inputs

### 1

Raw inputs that contain personal data (uploaded documents, free-text messages, chat transcripts,
application payloads) MUST be deleted or irreversibly anonymised no later than 30 days after
collection, unless a legal retention obligation applies and that obligation is documented in the
repository (record of processing or data retention schedule).

### 2

Deletion MUST be automated (scheduled job, storage lifecycle rule or database TTL). Manual deletion
procedures alone are not sufficient.

## POL-5 | Encryption at rest and in transit

### 1

All storage holding personal data, model artefacts or prediction logs MUST be encrypted at rest using
platform-managed or customer-managed keys (for example AWS KMS).

### 2

All network traffic to and between services MUST use TLS 1.2 or higher. Plain HTTP endpoints are only
allowed for local development.

## POL-6 | Access control on model endpoints

### 1

Inference and model-management endpoints MUST require authentication. Endpoints that are deliberately
public (for example a customer-facing chat widget) MUST be declared as public in the service
configuration and MUST be protected by rate limiting (POL-9).

### 2

Administrative endpoints (model reload, threshold changes, data export, transcript access) MUST
enforce role-based authorisation.

## POL-7 | Human approval for automated decisions with significant effect

### 1

An AI system MUST NOT autonomously take any decision that produces legal or similarly significant
effects for a natural person, whether adverse or favourable, such as shortlisting or rejecting a job
application, granting or declining credit, setting a credit limit, or closing an account, regardless
of the amount or volume involved. Such decisions MUST be approved by a trained human reviewer before
they are communicated or take effect.

### 2

The reviewer MUST be able to see the AI output, its main reasons, and MUST be able to change the
outcome. The approval MUST be recorded in the prediction log (POL-3).

### 3

Rejections and declines MUST be approved by two reviewers (four-eyes principle), at least one of whom
did not handle the case before.

## POL-8 | Model cards

### 1

Every model deployed to production MUST have a model card in the repository (`MODEL_CARD.md` or
`*.modelcard.md`) describing: intended use and out-of-scope uses, model version, training data
summary, evaluation metrics, known limitations, and the owning team. For third-party models the card
documents the provider, model version and the product-specific evaluation.

## POL-9 | Rate limiting on public AI endpoints

### 1

AI endpoints reachable from the internet or from untrusted networks MUST enforce rate limiting per
client (API key, session or IP), with limits defined in configuration, to contain abuse, scraping
and cost-exhaustion attacks.

## POL-10 | AI incident reporting

### 1

Each product team MUST maintain an AI incident response procedure in the repository describing how
AI incidents (harmful or discriminatory outputs, data leaks via the model, significant performance
degradation, safety events) are detected, escalated and reported.

### 2

Serious incidents MUST be reported to the AI Governance Board (ai-incidents@acme.example) within
24 hours of detection.

## POL-11 | Bias and fairness evaluation before release

### 1

Before any release of a model that is used to make or support decisions about natural persons, the
team MUST run and document a bias and fairness evaluation covering relevant protected
characteristics (or suitable proxies), including the metrics used, the results per group, and the
mitigations taken. The evaluation report MUST be stored in the repository and referenced in the model
card.

## POL-12 | Watermarking of generative AI output

### 1

Images, audio and video generated by an AI system MUST embed a robust watermark or a C2PA content
credential identifying the content as AI-generated, the generating model and the generation date.

### 2

Generated text delivered through an API MUST carry a machine-readable provenance field (for example
`"ai_generated": true` in the response body or an `X-AI-Generated` header). Removal of these markers
by downstream ACME services is prohibited.
